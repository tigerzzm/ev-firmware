Current steering — center-pivot beam axle (top view, from your CAD)

Proportions taken from the real parts: beam ≈ 95 mm wide, steering arm ≈ 75 mm long. Front of car = up.

How it works today: both front wheels sit on one rigid beam that pivots about a single vertical axis. A long steering arm off the beam is positioned to a spot you measure with the digital caliper and then locked — so the wheels hold one fixed angle and the car drives a fixed arc. It's rock-solid (zero play) precisely because it's locked.

The retrofit: the servo takes over that arm/pivot so the angle can be commanded live. Drive it coaxially at the pivot for a 1:1, lowest-slack connection, or drive the end of the ~75 mm arm for a built-in reduction (more torque + backlash divided down, but one extra joint). Which is better depends on which feature is the true pivot — tell me that (or send a photo) and I'll finalize it.
